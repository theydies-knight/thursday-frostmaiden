# Home Rule: Story Points

Story Points represent the shifting momentum of fate at the table. Rather than belonging to individual characters, they operate in a tug-of-war economy between the players and the Game Master.

## The Shared Pool

The party shares a communal pool with a maximum number of **Story Points** equal to the number of players at the table (for example, four players have a pool of four points). Any player may draw from this shared pool.

## Spending Story Points

You can spend **1 Story Point** to roll **1d4** and add the result to any attack roll, ability check, or saving throw you make. You can choose to spend a Story Point after rolling the d20, provided you declare it before the GM announces whether the roll succeeds or fails.

## Flipping to Doom

Story Points are never permanently expended—they change hands. Whenever a player spends a Story Point, that point immediately transfers to the GM's pool as a point of **Doom** (also referred to as an **Omen**).

The GM can spend points from their pool to enhance enemy rolls, introduce complications, or escalate the stakes of a scene. Whenever the GM spends a point of Doom, it immediately returns to the party's pool as an available Story Point.

# Home Rule: Loric Inspiration

During the debrief after each session, the players will have an opportunity to award Loric Inspiration to one Player Character. This permits the character to add a die to any attack roll, skill check, or saving throw. The die value scales similarly to a Bardic Inspiration die. A character may only have a single instance of Loric Inspiration at a time. 

| Levels | Die  |
| :----: | :---: |

|  5-9   | 1d8  |
| 10-14  | 1d10 |
| 15-20  | 1d12 |
# Heroic Inspiration

***Rules as Written.*** **Heroic Inspiration** may be used to reroll *any* single die. 

# Home Rule: Hit Dice

- General Rule: Reroll ones for hit dice rolled to restore HP at a short rest.
- On Level Up: If you roll less than half the your Hit Die value, take the average. (Generally equal to half the die value plus one.

# Death Saving Throws:

- Less than 10 is a failure,
- 10 or greater is a success,
- Nat 20 restores 1 HP, you are prone,
- Nat 1 counts for 2 failures,

# Rest Casting:

***Rules as Intended.*** Before taking a Long Rest, you may "Ready an Action" to cast long term spells when you start your day. (*Mage Armor, Gift of Alacrity, Hero's Feast, etc.*) You must have the appropriate spell slot available *before* resting. Rest Casting will allow you to benefit from these spells while benefitting from fully restored spell slots.

# Home Rule: Retreat

In a dire situation when the party decides that retreat is the best option, the party may decide to take a **Full Retreat**. If a retreat is not possible for an encounter, the DM must specify *before* initiative is rolled.

## In order to execute a retreat:

- A retreat can only be called during the turn of a conscious character who is under control of their faculties.
- At least half of the party must be able to take the dash action without being slowed by a condition or difficult terrain. Spells and abilities that increase movement speed or grant teleportation are permissible.
- Unconscious or immobile characters can be retrieved and carried within reason.

## Consequences of a retreat:

- All characters take 2 levels of exhaustion.
- All characters lose 20% of their material wealth, half their rations, and half their ammo. Spellcasters lose access to 2 of their highest level spell slots until all exhaustion is cleared.
- Story significant consequences.

# Home Rule: Cold Weather Gear Durability

In the brutal climate of Icewind Dale, survival depends as much on the integrity of an adventurer's layers as on their steel and magic. Biting winds, corrosive beasts, jagged ice, and violent combat degrade heavy parkas, sealskin boots, and snow goggles. 

Under this rule, **Cold Weather Clothing** tracks wear and tear on a **4-point Durability Track**.

### The Wear Track

Cold weather gear starts at **0 Wear** (Pristine). As the garments sustain damage from combat, harsh traversal, and environmental hazards, they accumulate **Wear Points** from 0 to 4:

| Wear Points | Condition       | Mechanical Effect                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| :---------: | :-------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
|    **0**    | **Pristine**    | The garment is intact. The wearer automatically succeeds on saving throws against extreme cold (as long as the clothing remains dry).                                                                                                                                                    |
|    **1**    | **Frayed**      | Scuffed leather, minor stitch tears, and surface frost-burn. **No mechanical penalty**, but visible cosmetic wear.                                                                                                                                                                       |
|    **2**    | **Compromised** | Significant seam splitting, thinning down/fur padding, or damaged toggles. The suit still functions, but further neglect risks failure. _(Optional: Disadvantage on Dexterity [Stealth] checks made in howling winds due to stiffened, flapping material)._                              |
|    **3**    | **Tattered**    | Massive tears, missing mittens/muffler, cracked goggles, or exposed under-layers. The garment no longer grants automatic success against extreme cold. The wearer must make the hourly **DC 10 Constitution saving throw against Extreme Cold**, but does so with **Advantage**.         |
|    **4**    | **Ruined**      | Shredded, soaked, or blown out beyond functioning insulation. The gear is **useless**. The wearer is treated as being without cold weather clothing, automatically subject to standard Extreme Cold rules (**DC 10 Constitution saving throw each hour or gain 1 level of exhaustion**). |

### Accumulating Wear

The Dungeon Master can assign Wear Points when gear is exposed to significant violence or extreme natural hazards:

1. **Combat Trauma:**
	- **Critical Hits:** When a creature wearing cold weather clothing suffers a critical hit from an attack dealing bludgeoning, piercing, slashing, acid, fire, or cold damage, their gear gains **1 Wear Point**.
	- **Massive Damage / Dropping to 0 HP:** Dropping to 0 hit points from attacks that shred, freeze, or burn the body inflicts **1 Wear Point**.
	- **Corrosive / Caustic Effects:** Exposure to continuous acid, magical fire, or necrotic rot (such as an ooze engulfing the target or an acid spray) inflicts **1–2 Wear Points**.
2. **Environmental & Travel Hazards:**
	- **Blizzard Travel & Avalanches:** Failing a group navigation or survival check while marching through a heavy blizzard or surviving an avalanche inflicts **1 Wear Point** on participants' gear.
	- **Submersion in Frigid Water:** Falling into freezing water immediately compromises the outfit. Until dried by a campfire over 1 hour, it ceases to protect against the cold and accumulates **1 Wear Point** from waterlogged freezing.

### Maintenance & Field Repairs

Adventurers can preserve their gear through rest downtime, magic, or town services.
#### 1. Camp Maintenance (Long Rest)

During a Long Rest, a character can spend 1 hour of light activity mending seams, reinforcing patches, and oiling leather:

- A character makes a **DC 12 Wisdom (Survival)** check (or an Intelligence check using _Cobbler’s Tools_ or _Leatherworker’s Tools_).
- **On a success:** Remove **1 Wear Point** from one set of gear. Exceeding the DC by 5 or more (DC 17+) removes **2 Wear Points**, or removes 1 point from two different party members' outfits.
- **Harvested Materials:** Characters who harvest fresh pelts or sinew from hunted game (such as elk, crag cat, or bear) gain **Advantage** on this check.
#### 2. The _Mending_ Cantrip

- Casting _Mending_ (casting time: 1 minute) seals torn seams, repairs broken goggles, or reattaches ripped furs.
- A single casting of _Mending_ during a rest repairs **1 Wear Point**. Because _Mending_ can only fuse breaks or tears no larger than 1 foot in diameter and cannot replace lost insulation or down, it can reduce an item's wear down to a minimum of **1 Point**. Fully restoring gear from 1 to 0 requires proper materials (pelts, thread, or outfitter servicing).
#### 3. Town Outfitters & Tailors

- Visiting a proper outfitter or smithy (such as _Blackiron Blades_ in Bryn Shander or _Frozenfar Expeditions_ in Caer-Konig) allows full refurbishment.
- For a fee of **2 gp** (or **5 gp** if the gear is at 3+ Wear), an artisan completely restores a damaged set of Cold Weather Clothing back to **0 Wear (Pristine)**. A completely destroyed set (beyond 4) must be replaced at full price (10 gp).
