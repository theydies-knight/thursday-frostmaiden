# Story Points

Story Points represent the shifting momentum of fate at the table. Rather than belonging to individual characters, they operate in a tug-of-war economy between the players and the Game Master.

## The Shared Pool

The party shares a communal pool with a maximum number of **Story Points** equal to the number of players at the table (for example, four players have a pool of four points). Any player may draw from this shared pool.

## Spending Story Points

You can spend **1 Story Point** to roll **1d4** and add the result to any attack roll, ability check, or saving throw you make. You can choose to spend a Story Point after rolling the d20, provided you declare it before the GM announces whether the roll succeeds or fails.

## Flipping to Doom

Story Points are never permanently expended—they change hands. Whenever a player spends a Story Point, that point immediately transfers to the GM's pool as a point of **Doom** (also referred to as an **Omen**).

The GM can spend points from their pool to enhance enemy rolls, introduce complications, or escalate the stakes of a scene. Whenever the GM spends a point of Doom, it immediately returns to the party's pool as an available Story Point.

# Loric Inspiration

During the debrief after each session, the players will have an opportunity to award Loric Inspiration to one Player Character. This permits the character to add a die to any attack roll, skill check, or saving throw. The die value scales similarly to a Bardic Inspiration die. A character may only have a single instance of Loric Inspiration. 

| Levels | Die  |
| :----: | :---: |
|  1-4   | 1d6  |
|  5-9   | 1d8  |
| 10-14  | 1d10 |
| 15-20  | 1d12 |

# Hit Dice

- General Rule: Reroll ones for hit dice rolled to restore HP at a short rest.
- On Level Up: If you roll less than half the your Hit Die value, take the average. (Generally equal to half the die value plus one.

# Death Saving Throws:

- Less than 10 is a failure,
- 10 or greater is a success,
- Nat 20 restores 1 HP, you are prone,
- Nat 1 counts for 2 failures,

# Rest Casting:

- Before taking a Long Rest, you may "Ready an Action" to cast long term spells when you start your day. (*Mage Armor, Gift of Alacrity, Hero's Feast, etc.*) You must have the appropriate spell slot available *before* resting. Rest Casting will allow you to benefit from these spells while benefitting from fully restored spell slots.

# Retreat

In a dire situation when the party decides that retreat is the best option, the party may decide to take a **Full Retreat**. If a retreat is not possible for an encounter, the DM must specify *before* initiative is rolled.

## In order to execute a retreat:

- A retreat can only be called during the turn of a conscious character who is under control of their faculties.
- At least half of the party must be able to take the dash action without being slowed by a condition or difficult terrain. Spells and abilities that increase movement speed or grant teleportation are permissible.
- Unconscious or immobile characters can be retrieved and carried within reason.

## Consequences of a retreat:

- All characters take 2 levels of exhaustion.
- All characters lose 20% of their material wealth, half their rations, and half their ammo. Spellcasters lose access to 2 of their highest level spell slots until all exhaustion is cleared.
- Story significant consequences.
